## Notes

avoid Kobayashi Maru.
If you are unaware of all possible actions you could take, you may be unable to solve the problem.
Restating the problem in a more precise and formal way is a great way of dealing with the problem itself.
Thinking about the problem brings more value than thinking about the solution.
It is better to make a plan than to solve a problem with trial and error.
We can often first fidget with a smaller/reduced version of the program and then advance.
When solving problems we must have a goal, even if we don't achieve it by the end. That is better than mindlessly going through it.
We firstly look for the most constrained part of the problem.
We must always recognize analogies(problems we have successfully solved already).
The more problems we solve, the more analogies we can see.
1. You should always have a plan(solve the problem in your head before you put it into code)

General Dwight D. Eisenhower: "I have always found that plans are useless, but planning is indispensable."

2. You should restate the problem if you don't understand it.
With a plan, the first step of the plan can be "formally restate the problem".

3. You should always divide your problems into sub-problems. This usually leads into easier progress.
4. Start with what you know and work outward from there.
This also implies in cases where you haven't divided the problem. Always try to solve the problem with the knowledge you have and only if you really can't try to seek other concepts or help.
5. If you don't know how to solve a problem, try to reduce it with removing constraints or even adding them.(so that you come to the problem you already know how to solve).
