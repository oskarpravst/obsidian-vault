# Conservative Force

## Definition

A force is **conservative** (*konzervativna*) when its [[Work|work]] depends only on the initial and final position, not on the path. Around any closed path its work is $0$.

## Explanation

- The work of a conservative force can be replaced by [[Potential Energy|potential energy]]: $\Delta W_p = -A$.
- The opposite are **dissipative forces** (*disipativne sile*), which oppose motion and always do negative work: [[Friction|friction]], static friction, air resistance. Their work depends on the path, so they have no potential energy.

| conservative | dissipative |
|---|---|
| gravitation / force of gravity | friction ($\vec{v} \neq 0$) |
| spring (elastic) force | static friction ($\vec{v} = 0$) |
| electric force | air resistance |

## Examples

**Force of gravity** (Physics 3): $A_g = -mg(h_k - h_z)$. Starting and finishing at the same height gives $A_g = 0$.

**Spring** (Physics 3): $A_{vz} = -\left(\frac{1}{2}kx_k^2 - \frac{1}{2}kx_z^2\right)$, depends only on the end positions.

**Electric force** (Physics 6): by analogy with gravitation it is conservative, so we introduce the electric potential energy $\Delta W_{ep} = -A_e$ and from it the potential and [[Voltage|voltage]].
