# Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation

## Definition

Two bodies with masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ at distance $r$ attract each other with the force

$$F_g = G\,\frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}, \qquad G \simeq 6{,}67 \cdot 10^{-11}\ \frac{\text{N}\,\text{m}^2}{\text{kg}^2}$$

The script writes the gravitational constant as $\kappa$ (*kapa*).

## Explanation

- The force is **always attractive** and acts along the line between the centres of mass. By Newton's 3rd law, both bodies feel forces of the same size.
- For spheres, $r$ is the distance between their centres (spherical symmetry).
- It is a [[Conservative Force|conservative force]], with [[Potential Energy|potential energy]] $W_g = -G\frac{m_1 m_2}{r}$ (zero at $r = \infty$).
- **On the Earth's surface** it is the force of gravity: $G\frac{M_Z m}{R_Z^2} = mg$. Higher up it falls as $F_g = mg\frac{R_Z^2}{r^2}$.
- **Inside the Earth** only the mass below you counts. A spherical shell exerts no total force on a body inside it ([[Shell Theorem|shell theorem]]), so at the centre of the Earth you would be weightless.
- **Analogy with electrostatics:** [[Coulomb's Law|Coulomb's law]] has the same $1/r^2$ form, but charges can also repel.
- **Limits:** Mercury's orbit and [[Gravitational Lensing|gravitational lensing]] are explained only by [[General Relativity|general relativity]], where mass curves space-time.

## Examples

**Cavendish** measured $G$ with a torsion balance (Physics 3).

**[[Kepler's Laws]]** (Physics 5): gravitation is a central force, so the angular momentum of a planet is conserved, which gives Kepler's 2nd law.
