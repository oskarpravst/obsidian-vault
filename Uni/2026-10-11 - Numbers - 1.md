## Notes

### OMA 1 — Natural numbers and induction

**Definition:** Natural numbers (*naravna števila*) $\mathbb{N} = \{1, 2, \dots\}$

**Tool** (*orodje*): the principle of induction (*princip indukcije*) — "dominoes".

Let $A \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ satisfy:

- $m \in A$
- $\forall k \in \mathbb{N}: k \in A \implies k + 1 \in A$

Then $\{m, m+1, \dots\} \subseteq A$.

**How to use it** (*shema uporabe*): we want to prove a statement $T(m)$ $\forall m \in \mathbb{N}$.

**Procedure** (*postopek*):

1. **Base case** (*baza indukcije*): prove $T(1)$.
2. **Induction step** (*indukcijski korak*): assume $T(k)$ holds and derive $T(k+1)$, $\forall k \in \mathbb{N}$.
3. **Conclusion** (*indukcijski sklep*): $T(m)$ holds $\forall m \in \mathbb{N}$.

#### Example 1

Prove $T(m)$: $\underbrace{1 + 2 + 3 + \dots + m}_{L(m)} = \underbrace{\dfrac{m(m+1)}{2}}_{D(m)}$, $\forall m \in \mathbb{N}$.

**1. Base case,** $m = 1$:

$$L(1) = 1, \qquad D(1) = \frac{1(1+1)}{2} = 1$$

$$L(1) = D(1) \implies T(1) \text{ holds}$$

**2. Induction step,** $m \rightsquigarrow m + 1$:

Assume $L(m) = D(m)$ (*induction hypothesis*, I.H. — *indukcijska predpostavka*).

$$L(m+1) = \underbrace{1 + 2 + \dots + (m-1) + m}_{L(m)} + (m+1) = L(m) + (m+1)$$

$$\overset{\text{I.H.}}{=} \frac{m(m+1)}{2} + \frac{2(m+1)}{2} = \frac{(m+1)(m+2)}{2} = D(m+1) \ \checkmark$$

**Conclusion** (*sklep*): $L(m) = D(m)$ $\forall m \in \mathbb{N}$, i.e. $T(m)$ holds.

**Homework** (*doma*): $1^2 + 2^2 + \dots + m^2 = \dfrac{m(m+1)(2n+1)}{6}$

**Exercise** (*naloga*): Suppose $T(3)$ holds and $T(k) \implies T(k+3)$. Can we conclude that $T(2026)$ holds?

### Integers, rational, real and irrational numbers

- **Integers** (*cela števila*): $\mathbb{Z} = \{\dots, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, \dots\}$ — closed under $+$, $-$.
- **Rational numbers** (*racionalna števila*): $\mathbb{Q} = \{\frac{p}{q};\ p, q \in \mathbb{Z},\ q \neq 0\}$ — closed under $+$, $-$, $\cdot$, $:$ (except division by $0$).
  - $x^2 = -5$ has no solution (→ complex numbers).
- **Real numbers** (*realna števila*): $\mathbb{R}$ … all points on the line, i.e. all "ends" of intervals in $\mathbb{Q}$.
  - They have a decimal representation, which is not always unique: $0.999\ldots = 1$.
  - Example: $\{x : x^2 < 2\}$ in $\mathbb{Q}$ — its "end" is undefined in $\mathbb{Q}$ (in $\mathbb{R}$ it is defined).
- **Irrational numbers** (*iracionalna števila*): $\mathbb{R} \setminus \mathbb{Q}$ or $\mathbb{R} - \mathbb{Q}$ (set difference — *razlika množic*), e.g. $\pi$, $\sqrt{2}$, …

### Bounds, supremum and infimum

**Definition:** Let $A \subseteq \mathbb{R}$, $A \neq \emptyset$ (empty set — *prazna množica*).

1. $A$ is **bounded above** (*navzgor omejena*) if $\exists M \in \mathbb{R}: \forall m \in A$ holds $m \leq M$. Such $M$ is called an **upper bound** (*zgornja meja*).
2. If $A$ is bounded above, the least upper bound always exists (in $\mathbb{R}$) and is called the [[Supremum and Infimum|supremum]] $[\sup(A)]$.
3. If $\sup(A) \in A$, we call it the **maximum** $[\max(A)]$.

| $A$ | $\sup$ | $\max$ |
|---|:-:|:-:|
| $(0, 5)$ | $5$ | — |
| $(1, 3]$ | $3$ | $3$ |
| $(5, \infty)$ | — | — |
| $(-\infty, 5]$ | $5$ | $5$ |

**Homework** (*DN*): analogous definitions for *bounded below*, *lower bound* (infimum, $\inf(A)$) and *minimum* ($\min(A)$):

1. $A$ is **bounded below** (*navzdol omejena*) if $\exists m \in \mathbb{R}: \forall n \in A$ holds $m \leq n$. Such $m$ is called a **lower bound** (*spodnja meja*).
2. If $A$ is bounded below, the greatest lower bound always exists (in $\mathbb{R}$) and is called the **infimum** $[\inf(A)]$.
3. If $\inf(A) \in A$, we call it the **minimum** $[\min(A)]$.

### Absolute value (*absolutna vrednost*)

**Definition:** for $x \in \mathbb{R}$, the absolute value of $x$ is

$$|x| = \begin{cases} x; & x \geq 0 \\ -x; & x < 0 \end{cases}$$

— the distance from $x$ to $0$.

**Properties** (*lastnosti*):

- $|x - y|$ … the distance between $x$ and $y$
- $|x| \geq 0$
- $|x| \cdot |y| = |x \cdot y|$
- $|x + y| \leq |x| + |y|$

#### Example 1: $|x - 1| = |x + 3|$

Split by the sign of each expression:

- $x - 1 \geq 0 \iff x \geq 1$: $\ |x - 1| = x - 1$; otherwise $-(x - 1)$
- $x + 3 \geq 0 \iff x \geq -3$: $\ |x + 3| = x + 3$; otherwise $-(x + 3)$

This divides the line at $-3$ and $1$ into three intervals:

- $x \leq -3$: $\ -(x - 1) = -(x + 3) \implies 1 = -3$ — **no solution**
- $x \geq 1$: $\ x - 1 = x + 3 \implies -1 = 3$ — **no solution**
- $x \in (-3, 1)$: $\ -(x - 1) = x + 3 \implies x = -1$ — check ✓

**Solution:** $x = -1$

### Complex numbers $\mathbb{C}$ (*kompleksna števila*)

We introduce a new number $i$ with the property $i^2 = -1$.

$$\mathbb{C} = \{x + i \cdot y;\ x, y \in \mathbb{R}\}$$

$z = x + iy$, where

- $x$ = real part (*realna komponenta*), $\operatorname{Re}(z)$
- $y$ = imaginary part (*imaginarna komponenta*), $\operatorname{Im}(z)$

**Model:** the complex plane (*kompleksna ravnina*) — $x$ on the horizontal axis, $y$ on the vertical axis. The sketch shows $2 + i$, its distance $|z|$ from the origin, $-z$, and $\bar{z} = 2 - i$.

#### Arithmetic in $\mathbb{C}$ (*računanje v* $\mathbb{C}$)

1. $(a + ib) \pm (c + id) = (a \pm c) + i(b \pm d)$
2. $(a + ib) \cdot (c + id) = ac + iad + ibc + i^2 bd = (ac - bd) + i(ad + bc)$
3. Division — multiply by the conjugate of the denominator:
   $$\frac{1 + 2i}{1 + i} \cdot \frac{1 - i}{1 - i} = \frac{1 + 2 + i}{1 + 1} = \frac{3 + i}{2} = \frac{3}{2} + i\frac{1}{2}$$
   Another example of the final form: $\dfrac{3 + 4i}{5} = \dfrac{3}{5} + i\dfrac{4}{5}$

#### Conjugate and modulus

**Definition:** for $z = x + iy$, the **complex conjugate** (*konjugirano število*) of $z$ is $\bar{z} = x - iy$.

$$|z|^2 = z \cdot \bar{z} = (x + iy)(x - iy) = x^2 - (iy)^2 = x^2 + y^2 \in \mathbb{R}$$

$|z|$ is the absolute value (modulus) of $z$.

**Properties of $|z|$:**

1. $|\bar{z}| = |z|$
2. $|z_1 \cdot z_2| = |z_1| \cdot |z_2|$
3. $|z_1 + z_2| \leq |z_1| + |z_2|$ — **triangle inequality** (*trikotniška neenakost*)

**Properties of $\bar{z}$:**

1. $\bar{\bar{z}} = z$
2. $\overline{z_1 + z_2} = \bar{z}_1 + \bar{z}_2$, $\quad \overline{z_1 \cdot z_2} = \bar{z}_1 \cdot \bar{z}_2$
3. $x = \dfrac{z + \bar{z}}{2}$, $\quad y = \dfrac{z - \bar{z}}{2i}$

#### Example 1: $2\bar{z} = z^2$

Let $z = x + iy$:

$$2(x - iy) = (x + iy)(x + iy)$$

$$2x - 2iy = x^2 - y^2 + 2ixy$$

- Real part: $2x = x^2 - y^2$
- Imaginary part: $-2y = 2xy$ → $x = -1$ or $y = 0$

**Case $x = -1$:** $\ -2 = 1 - y^2 \implies y^2 = 3 \implies y = \pm\sqrt{3}$
Solution: $z = -1 \pm i\sqrt{3}$

**Case $y = 0$:** $\ 2x = x^2 \implies x^2 - 2x = 0 \implies x(x - 2) = 0 \implies x_1 = 0,\ x_2 = 2$
Solution: $z = 0$, $z = 2$

**All solutions:** $z \in \{0,\ 2,\ -1 + i\sqrt{3},\ -1 - i\sqrt{3}\}$
